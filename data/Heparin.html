HEPARIN
INDICATIONS AND DOSES
In most situations, LMWH is preferable to IV heparin.

Prophylaxis
LMWH
Medical Patients
All other than those with minor medical illness:
    40mg sc nocte for 6-14 days
Surgical Patients
Moderate risk patients (>30min op and >40 years, cancer, high dose estrogen therapy, epidural):
    20mg sc nocte
    Commenced the night before surgery and continued until fully mobilising
High risk patients (previous DVT/PE, pelvic surgery, known thrombophilia, >2h op, ortho surgery of pelvis, hip or lower limb, multiple trauma):
    40mg sc nocte

Treatment
DVT or PE
Heparin is used until a therapeutic level of anticoagulation has been achieved with warfarin.

LMWH
1mg/kg body weight sc q12h or 1.5mg/kg sc q24h
(In obese patients or patients with baseline iliac vein thrombosis or cancer the former  may be more beneficial).

Unfractionated Heparin:
Target APTT: 70-90s
Bolus: 5000-10000 units diluted in 5ml normal saline as a slow IV push over 1-2 mins.
Infusion:  25000 units in 45mls normal saline (total volume 50mls of 500 units/ml).  Commenced immediately after bolus at 1250 units/hour.
Measure APTT at 6 hourly intervals until therapeutic and stable, then daily measures (usually each morning) for duration of heparin therapy).

PHYSICOCHEMICAL PROPERTIES
Heparin is a glycosaminoglycan (a polysaccharide chain with lots of glucoronic acid and glucosamine residues), normally produced in the body by mast cells.
The therapeutic variety is purified from porcine gut and bovine lung mucosa.

Two main types exist:
Unfractionated heparin
Contains a range of lengths of polylsaccharide chains (5-30kDa)

Low molecular weight heparin
Contains only short polysaccharide chains (1-10kDa)

PHARMACODYNAMICS
Heparin increases binding of antithrombin (factor III, a normal circulating inhibitor of coagulation) to thrombin (unfractionated heparin) or factor Xa (LMWH) inactivating them (see Coagulation Cascade).  This inhibits conversion of fibrinogen to fibrin, thus slowing coagulation.
PHARMACOKINETICS
Administration:  Either IV or subcutaneous injection.
Can be used during pregnancy because it is too large to cross the placenta.

Unfractionated heparin
Disadvantages:
  • Variable kinetics because of the range of different length polysaccharide chains.  Requires monitoring by APTT.
  • IV administration
Advantages:
  • Rapid onset.
  • Effect lasts 1-3 hours thus can be stopped suddenly.
LMWH
Disadvantages:
  • Effect lasts 12 hours and thus cannot be reversed suddenly.
  • Longer onset of action
Advantages:
  • Predictable bioavailability thus can be given on a mg/kg basis and does not require monitoring (and cannot be monitored using the APTT because LMWH prevents interaction of antithrombin with Xa rather than thrombin).
  • SC injection can be given to outpatients.

PRECAUTIONS
Recent major surgery

LP/Epidural
Placement or removal of an epidural or spinal needle or catheter should be delayed at least 10-12 hours after administration of a DVT prophylactic dose and >24 hours after a treatment dose.  Subsequent dose should be withheld for >2 hours after the procedure.

Severe renal impairment
LMWH dose should be reduced:
    1mg/kg bd is reduced to 1mg/kg od
    1.5mg/kg od is reduced to 1mg/kg od

Advanced age
LMWH dose should be reduced:
    0.75mg/kg sc bd

Thrombocytopaenia
Occurs in 1-5% of those treated for >1 week.  Reversible if heparin is stopped.  Lower risk with LMWH.

Osteoporosis
Can occur with long term use.
