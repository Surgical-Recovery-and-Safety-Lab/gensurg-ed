BIOLOGICAL BEHAVIOUR

Pathophysiology

Combination of:
-Insulin deficiency
- Stressor (increases adrenaline)
- Increased glucagon (occurs secondary to low insulin)

Results in:
Maximal gluconeogenesis
- due to high gluagon
Impaired use of glucose
- caused by low insulin

1. Severe hyperglycaemia
--> osmotic diuresis
--> volume depletion
- in known diabetics, a vicious cycle may begin with poor circulation causing poor absorption of SC insulin.

2. Ketogenesis activated
- because body can't use glucose, initiates lipolysis
- FFA release in the presence of glucogen excess causes accelerated ketogenesis, due to mitochondrial physiology.
- acetoacetate and B-hydroxybuterate released

3. Metabolic Acidosis
pH falls due to above acids

4. Electrolyte Disturbance
Potassium stores down
- often several hundred mmol
- lost into urine in ketosis.
- however serum levels high initally as swapped out of cells by free H+
Serum sodium low
- water drawn out of cells osmotically by glucose.
- nb high TriGs may distort low level in DKA.

Complications
Cerebral oedema
Seen in teenagers/kids especially
Due to overzealous correction of BSL.
Consider if declining consciousness despite improving biochemistry.
- monitor consciousness
- undertake fluid replacement slowly

INVESTIGATIONS
Biochemistry
BSLs
Urine test for ketones.
DKA defined as:
- ph<7.3 and/or bicarb <15mmol/L
- +ve ketones in urine
- hyperglycaemia

Other
Look for the precipitating cause.
- eg CXR, ECG, CBC, Trop, septic screen.
High amylase may occur due to extra-pancreatic secretion, interpret cautiously (though frank pancreatitis may occur with DKA).

MANAGEMENT

Emergency

1. ABCs.
- maintain airway if pt unable.
- NG tube if unable to protect airway.
- check vital signs ?shock

2. Urgent BSL
(pretreatment)

3. Urgent ABG
- venous gas may be ok if mild DKA only
- if pH<7.1 consider ICU.

4. Monitoring
-  start a flow chart for fluid balance, pH, electrolytes, BSLs, and insulin received.
Electrolytes
- hourly if severly ill
- drop to 2-4hrly after 4hrs.
Vitals
- similar or higher frequency

5. Fluids
Urgently.
- hypovolaemic shock possible
- often large requirement due to diuresis
Amt depends on clinical judgement
Common regimen:
- 1L of 0.9% saline q1hr.
- 500ml over second hr
- 500ml q2-4hrly thereafter.
- restore euvolaemia over 24-48hrs, using D5W when BSL hits 15mmol/L.
- catheterise to monitor
Care in cardiac or renal patients.
- may require CVP monitoring.
Care in adolescents / kids
- use 0.45% saline only after discussion with consultant

6.  Insulin
Urgently
- stat bolus ~5u IV actrapid.
IV infusion pump
- not SC as variable absorption
- particularly if shocked.
- use 50u Actrapid in 50ml 0.9S in a 50mL syringe
Short acting insulin.
Don't reduce glucose too quickly
- else  brain oedema may ensue.
- around 3mmol/hr reduction per hour is ok
- slow down rate when BSL hits ~20mmol/L.
 - some recommend reduce to normal over 24hrs.

7. Potassium
Less urgent than fluids & insulin
May initially be high in serum
- reduced in range of 10mmol/kg
K+ will fall quickly with treatments above
- follows glucose back into cells when insulin given
- as acidosis corrected, H+ migrates out of cells into serum via K+/H+ exchanger.
Monitor the serum K+ regularly
- start K+ replacement when serum level falls into normal range
- usually in the first hour of treatment.
- 20mmol over an hour is a good ballpark figure.

8. Treat underlying cause
Investigate as above
As per clinical suspicion.

9. Changing to SC Insulin
Eg Penmix 30.
Do this when
- acidosis resolved
- pt eating.
Proved suitable overlap to ensure SC insulin onset.

Pitfalls:
1. Not seeking senior help early.
2. Striving for rapid glucose and acidosis control.
- this will not kill them like hypovolaemia, electrolyte disturbance or coma will.
3. Not monitoring K+ closely
4. Commencing K+ replacement too late
5. Using SC insulin (variable absoprtion)
6. Underestimating fluid deficit
