PHARMACODYNAMICS
Warfarin is a vitamin K antagonist which blocks the vitamin K dependent gamma-carboxylation of glutamate residues important in the synthesis of four clotting factors in the liver (see Coagulation Cascade).  

I.e. Factors II, VII, IX and X which are activated in the liver by carboxylation of glutamic acid residues, a reaction which requires vitamin K.

These factors are important in both the intrinsic and extrinsic pathways:

Warfarin takes 8-12 hours to work because it only acts on newly produced clotting factors.  Clotting factors already in the circulation need to be cleared.

PHARMACOKINETICS
Administration: Oral
Absorption: Good
Onset: Delayed 1-3 days because the antithrombotic effect of warfarin is not fully expressed until the prothrombin concentration, which has a half-life of approximately 96 hours is suppressed to therapeutic levels.  Doses of warfarin that are too high too soon risk causing a prothrombotic state caused by transient depletion of protein C, a vitamin K-dependednt protein with intrinsic anticoagulant and fibrinolytic effects.  
Half life: 36 hours
Metabolism: Hepatic
Excretion: Renal

ADVERSE EFFECTS
Bleeding
Risk increases significantly if the INR is >5.0.  

Reversal of Warfarinisation
High INR (>5.0, <9.0), with no bleeding or minor bleeding
  • Withdraw warfarin for a few days
  • Give 1-2.5mg Vitamin K if there are risk factors for haemorrhage (e.g. history of bleeding, PUD, intracranial bleed, >70 years, epistaxis), give 1.0-2.5mg oral Vit K.  If INR is still high after 24 hours give a repeat dose of Vit K.
  • Re-check INR in 6-12hrs
  • Restart warfarin at a reduced dose when the INR is <5.0

Very high INR (>9.0), no bleeding
  • Stop warfarin and give 5mg Vit K.
  • Repeat INR in 6-12 hours.
  • Give coag factor replacement if there is high risk of bleeding.
  • Restart warfarin only when the INR is <5.0.

Major bleeding at any level of INR
  • Stop warfarin
  • FFP alone - 12-15ml or more/kg
  • Or: prothrombin complex concentrate (PCC) 50iu Factor IX/kg. (Contains Factor II, IX and X, but not VII).  Give with a slow infusion of 3ml/min of FFP to replace Factor VII.
  • Or: 5mg Vit K by slow IV injection with PCC and/or FFP.

INTERACTIONS
Metabolised by cytochrome P450 therefore has lots of interactions.

Metabolism induced by:
  • Phenytoin
  • Carbamazepine
  • Rifampicin
  • Phenobarbitone
Metabolism of the R isomer inhibited by:
  • Cimetidine
  • Omeprazole
    Monitor INR

Metabolism of the S isomer inhibited by:
  • Metronidazole
  • Co-trimoxazole
    Reduce dose

Metabolism of R and S isomer inhibited by
  • Amiodarone
