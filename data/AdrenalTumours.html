BIOLOGICAL BEHAVIOUR

Virilizing / Feminizing Tumours
Relatively uncommon but often (80%) malignant.
Present with hirsuitism in women, irregular mesnses, alopecia.
Have elevated testosterone, DEA-S and 24h-urinary 7-hydroxysteroids and 17-ketosteroids.
Rarely, there may be estrogen-secreting adrenal tumours (possibly carcinomas)
- may cause gynecomastia, gonadal atrophy, impotence, or loss of libido.
--> routine physical should also look for testicular tumours.
Treatment
Lap, converted to open if malignant features arise.

Adrenocortical Carcinoma
Rare; 1% of adrenal lesions, 1-2 per million.
Median age of diagnosis is 44y.
Potentially curable if caught early
- but most are dx late; only 30% confined to gland.
Majority will present with features of Cushing's syndrome
- remainder have large retroperitoneal masses without hormone overactivity (subtle defects would show on sophisticated urinary analyses not routinely done).
Concern for adrenal neoplasm should always be raised for any lesion >6cm

Adrenal Mets
Mainly lung (esp small cell), renal, melanoma, stomach, liker, oesophagus and breast.
CT features include
- irregular borders
- haemorrhage or tumour necrosis
- ROI >20HU on unenhanced CT
FDG-PET may be helpful.
FNA may help to confirm dx but only after phaeo has been excluded
Resection in highly selected patients.

INVESTIGATIONS

Virilizing Tumours
CT with thin slices
If an ovarian mass is present, dexamethasone suppression test to suppress androgens and ketosteroids from ovarian tumours but not adrenal

Adrenocortical Carcinoma
CT and MRI both demonstrate the lesion and help dx cancer
But MRI best, as shows also extension into renal vein and IVC
- invasion into IVC and presence of thrombus are 'local invasion' and are not contraindications to resection

Diagnostic Workup for ACC

1. Glucocorticoids (minimum of 3-4 tests)
- dexamethasone supression test (1 mg/ 23hr)
- excretion of free urinary cortisol (24-h urine)
- basal cortisol (serum)
- basal ACTH (plasma)

2. Sex steroids
- DHEA-S
- 17-OH-progesterone
- Androstenedione (serum)
- Testosterone (serum)
- 17B estradiol (serum)

3. Mineralocorticoids
- K+
- aldosterone / renin ratio (only when hypertensive and hypokalaemic)

4. Phaeochromocytoma (minimum 1 of 3)
- catecholamine excretion (24h urine)
- metanephrine excretion (24h urine)
- plasma metanephrines and normetanephrines

5. Imaging
- CT or MRI of abdomen and thorax
- Bone scintigraphy if suspected skeletal mets
- FDG-PET (optional)

Prognostic Factors

1. Completeness of excision

2. Tumour Stage
- T1 <5cm, no invasion
- T2 >5cm, no invasion
- T3, any size, local invasion
- T4, Any size, invading local organs.
- N1 positive nodes
- M1 = distant mets

Stage 1 = T1N0M0
Stage 2 = T2N0M0
Stage 3 = T1N1M0, T2N1,M0, T3N0M0
Stage 4 = T3N1M0 and greater

5-yr survival 50-60% for Stage 1-2,
25% for III,
10% 1yr survival for IV

3. Immunohistochemistry
- Ki67 expression associated with poorer outcome
MANAGEMENT

Adrenocortical Carcinoma

1. Extensive workup as above
- guide peri-operative management
- and serve as follow-up tumour markers.

2. Surgery
Open often preferred for large tumours; need for careful ne-bloc resection
Take locally involved organs if necessary
- sometimes even need thoraco-abdominal approach for tumours involving the diaphragm or IVC


Adjuvant therapy?
Mitotane
- pesticide analog that acts on adrenals.
- used if tumour large (e.g. >8cm), aggressive, but limited by GI and neuro toxicity.
Cisplatin
- useful in selected patients
Radiotherapy
Used for tumours with R1 or R2 resection, or with Stage III+ disease or high Ki67
