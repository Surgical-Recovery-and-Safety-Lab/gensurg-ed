Stomach Anatomy & Physiology Notes
Anatomy

Cardia, fundus, corpus, antrum (nerves of Laterjet = crow's food)
3 muscle layers

Histology
- cardiac gland area = mucus secreting cells, few pareital
- parietal gland area = deep 'gastric pits', chief cells at bottom (peptic), parietal cells centrally, mucus at top, then an epithelial duct
- pyloric gland area = distal 30%, contains G cells (make gastrin).

Blood Supply
LC = left gastric and right gastric (off common hepatic).
- in 60%, a posterior gastric a. arises off the middle third of the splenic and meets the posterior body.
GC = r. gastroepiploic (off gastroduodenal) and l. gastroepiploic (off splenic)
Fundus of GC = vasa brevia (short gastrics) off splenic and L. gastroepiploic.

Veins
To coronary, gastroepiploic, veins, splenic veins and ultimately portal veins.

Lymph
Parallels arteries; partially determines spread of gastric Ca.

Nerves
Because stomach rotates during embryology, R vagus supplies posterior stomach, L vagus anterior stomach.
- 90% sensory afferent, 10% efferent.
At GO Jx:
- anterior trunk sends a banch to liver through lesser omentum
- posterior trunk supplies coeliac plexus, i.e. parasympathetic innervation as far as transverse colon.
- some posterior branches track with the L. gastric artery after passing through the coeliac plexus.
- preganglionic fibres synapse with ganglion cells in Auerbach's plexus.
- postganglionic (cholinergic) fibres SM layers / mucosa.

Physiology

Volume of empty stomach is only 50mL
Receptive relaxation is mediated by vagal reflexes and abolished by vagotomy
Osmolality and chemical make-up of chyme is minotired by vagal receptors; vagal reflex delays emptying.
Gastrin also delays emptying; only hormone known to affect emptying.

Gastric Juice
About 500-1500 mL per day + 1L for each meal.
- Mucus - glycoproteins; impedes diffusion of pepsin and H+ and lubrcates.
- Pepsionegen (chief cells) = induced by cholinergic, also hormones (gastrin, secretin); activated below pH 5; best at 2; destroyed at 8.
- IF (parietal cells) - binds to B12; stimulated by same stuff as promots H+ from parietals.
- Blood group antigens = 75% secrete; reduced risk of duodenal ulcer
H+ (parietal cells) = Na+ / H+ exchange. Cl- remains near 150 meq/L; gastric juice is isotonic

Acid Control
Parietal cells transfer out HCO3- to balance H+ output
Secretion achieved via H+/K+ exchange ATPase (proton pump) (K+ and Cl- then leak out together, making it a HCl secreted acid).
Stimulated by histamine, gastrin and acetylcholine
cephalic phase - vagal stim - parietal cells
gastric phase - gastrin release
intestinal phase - food in duodenum promotes acid - hormonal

Inhibition
- pH <2.5 inhibits gastrin; blocked at 1.2
- somatostatin is inhibitory (paracrine)
- food in duodenum; esp fat (blocks both gastrin and acid secretion).

Protection
Gastric surface secretes mucus and HCO3-
- HCO3- stimulated by cAMP, prostaglandins, cholinomimetics, glucagon, CCK, paracrine.
- NSAIDS inhibit HCO3-, bile acids, ethanol, acetazolamide, alpha-adrenergic agonists.
- mucus is an unstirred layer, maintains a higher pH beneath, diffusion barrier;
- mucus is made and depleted (by pepsin) at ~the same rate; stimulated by prostaglandins; inhibited by NSAIDs

Integrated Fx
Food chewed, mixed with salivary amylase and lipase
Serum gastrin increases from 50-200 pg/mL 30 min after a meal
Food in antrum well mixed and becomes acidic
Peptic digestion 10% complete, carbs 30-40%, fat initiated

Gastric emptying takes <3 hrs for an average lean meal, can be 10hrs for a very fatty meal.

Hormones
Gastrin released from G cells in the antrum, duodenum, and pancreas, stimulates the secretion of acid and pepsin release.
CCK decreases gut motility and stimulates bile & pancreatic hormone release.
Secretin released in response to acid in the duodenum - tightens pylorus and slows gastric emptying, increases bicarbonate secretion and decreases acid.
VIP stimulates small gut and pancreatic secretion and vasodilation, inhibits gastric secretion.
