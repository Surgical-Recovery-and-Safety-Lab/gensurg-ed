Local Anaesthetic
Basics
1. To prolong shelf life and maintain electroneutrality, all are suspended in a weak acid
- it is this acidity that causes the burning on insertion.
2.Analgesia is not the same as anaesthetic (loss of all sensation)
- pain is carried in small non-myelinated C fibres, while larger nerves carry sensation and motor function.
- thus pain is easily blocked, touch and pressure require much greater doses.

Vasoconstrictors
Decreases bleeding and absorption
- hence increases potency, prolongs effect and decreases toxicity.
Never use in fingers / nose / penis / toes / ears.
- risk of tissue necrosis.

Alkalization
Sodium bicarb (add 1cc of 8.4% NaHCO3 to 10cc of lidocaine or mepivacaine or 20cc of bupivarcaine)
Decreases the burning pain
Speeds onset but theoretically increases absorption rate / side effects.

Toxicity
High plasma concentrations are dangerous.
- CNS most sensitive : light headed, paraesthesias, blurred vision / tinnitus; later disorientation, shivering twitches, seizures; at worst resp arrest and coma.
- cardiovascular: prolonged conduction time, prolonged PR, wide QRS; later heart block, bradycardia; bupivacaine can be arrhythmic, esp in the pregnant
- note, cardio effects exacerbated by hypoxia.
- allergies: rarely anaphylactic (reportable), usually due to preservatives; discontinue, use an antihistamine and observe carefully.
Technical Notes

Pre-incision or Post-incision
Pre-incision = a very good idea.
This inhibits build-up of nociceptive molecules that augment the post-op pain response
If you do not do this you will get amplification and prolongation of post-operative pain, hurting your patients unnecessarily.
(Supported by several trials).

Subdermal infiltration
Run in sub-dermal plane, 25g needle is fine.
- blunts subdermic nerve endings, reducing pain for dermal injection.
Slide forward while injecting
- reduces chances of intravascular injection,
Then block dermis, raising the skin wheal.
Infiltration Methods

Local
Most useful with de-novo incisions
Avoid infected or contaminated traumatic wounds

Field Block
Place around rather than at the site (rhomboid with midline the planned incision site).
Remains for longer: longer acting.
- ie less direct escape and absorption when incision not over infiltration site.
Can include deeper tissues by progressively changing the infiltration angle.
Especially useful for cysts and abscesses
Avoids oedema and distortion of the operation site.
Vasoconstrictor use is beneficial.
Optimal use enhanced by knowing the innervation of a site.

Nerve blocks
Take for example the intercostal block
- great analgesia but impairs the muscles (risky in COPD pts).
Digital nerve block:
- inject 1cc of 1% lignocaine directly into the web space
- aim just dorsal to the palmar/dorsal skin junction
- then redirect in palmar direction and inject another 1cc.
- then place a third 1 cc in a more dorsal direction.
- then repeat on the opposite side.
Median block:
- between FCR and PL.
- inject 2-3cm proximal to the wrist crease
- advance through flexor retinaculum then slowly inject 3ml.
Radial block:
- locate the EPL
- insert just superficial to tendon, infect 2cc, then advance into snuffbox, injecting another 1cc.
Ulnar block:
- direct medially from radial side of FCU tendon (lies beneath); inject 3-5cc.
Dose

Lignocaine: 3mg/kg (5-7mg/kg if adrenaline-containing)
Bupivacaine 0.5%: 2mg/kg.
