5.6: GI Tract

General Features

Originated in yolk sac, endoderm tissue.  A tube of muscle lined with mucous membrane.

Muscular wall: inner circular, outer longitudinal (really 2 spiral layers, inner being close-wound), allowing peristaltic action.  Is smooth muscle

Mucous membrane has epithelium, lamina propria, and muscularis mucosa, with scattered endocrine cells (produce peptides / amines for neurotransmission / hormones).  Are APUD cells.

Oesophagus

Stratified squamous non-keratinising epithelium, thickish muscularis mucosae, with small groups of mucous-secreting glands at the upper and lower ends.

Stomach

Abrupt change to single-layer columnar epithelium, continues to anal canal (though differs)

-           epithelium not flat, but dips into lamina propria to form glands, gastric pits in stomach to peptic and parietal cells (pepsin and HCl respectively); also intrinsic factor from parietals.

-           At cardia (for ~1cm) glands are shorter and cells secrete mucus.

-           In pylorus, cells like coiled test-tubes and again all mucus secreting; G cells (gastrin) and D cells (somatostatin) are also found in this area.

-           Enterochromaffin cells in body and pyloris secrete serotonin and endorphin.

Small Intestine

Columnar epithelium dips into glands (Crypts of Lieberkuhn) and thrown up into villi (soft-tissue cores covered in epithelium).  Some secrete mucus, others absorb (are enterocytes).

-           granular Paneth cells at bases of crypts secrete lysosyme

-           enteroendocrine cells in crypts secrete hormones, eg secretin, somatostatin and CCK (ECs too)

-           duodenum also has BrunnerÕs glands (mucus secreting)

-           PeyerÕs patches in the terminal ileum has groups of lymphoid follicles in the mucosa.

Large Intestine

No villi, many glands and goblet cells.  Stratified squamous again in anal canal.

Lower Oesophagus

Anatomical Relations

Normally 3cm of oesophagus lies within abdomen; is retroperitoneal but peritoneum invests anterior and L lateral aspects only (257) (runs to diaphragm on R as uppermost lesser omentum).  Lies on L crus, with aorta behind that. (253)

-           anterior and posterior trunks of vagus are related to relative oesophageal surfaces.

-           L lobe of liver is anterior (grooved), caudate lobe to R. (258)

Enters stomach at cardiac orifice, right margin continuous with lesser curve.  Left makes an acute angle (of His = 30-70¡); height of fundus above this angle = 2-5cm.

Lower oesophageal sphincter: no morphological thickening to correspond with measurable zone of ­P.   Guarding against reflux assisted by i) R crus fibre contraction; ii) entry angle of oesophagus; iii) long folds of oesophageal mucosa; iv) high pressure zone in lower oesophagus, v) positive intra-abdominal pressure. 

Blood Supply

Cardia & lower oesophagus mainly from below by ³1 branch of L gastric and branches of splenic artery (282-3)

Nerve Supply (301)

Vagal inhibitory fibres ¨ sphincter relaxation (swallow, belching, vomiting).

Vagal excitatory fibres ¨ resting sphincter tone. 

Run within the muscle layer \ skeletonisation of lower oesophagus doesnÕt disrupt LOS function. 

Sympathetic adrenergic fibres ¨ relaxation, ?function.

Stomach (258-60)

Muscular bag fixed at both ends with great size variability.

Divided into arbitrary sections (Pl 258):

Cardia (most fixed) where oesophagus enters; T10 level, 2.5cm L of midline, 40cm from teeth; interface with oesophagus mucosa is irregular, 1-2cm into oesophagus (ÔZ-line)

Fundus projects above level of entry of oesophagus in contact with diaphragm (air inside)

Body (ends on lesser curve at angular notch / incisure, seen in vivo where crowÕs foot fans out ¨ 45¡ line downwards to greater curve)

Pylorus is the antrum, then pyloric canal ˆ duodenal junction; to R of midline at L1 level; thickened as pyloric sphincter marked by prepyloric vein of Mayo; on head/neck of pancreas.

Structure (261)

Body mainly parietal & chief cells, antrum mainly G cells (make gastrin) but tongue of antral mucosa extends up lesser curve variable distance.

Outer longitudinal coat & inner circular/oblique coat completely invest stomach.  Thickest in antrum. 

-           reinforced by innermost oblique, loops over fundus (thick at notch) to maintain acute angle

Pylorus

Circular muscle of distal antrum thickens to form 2 loops:  proximal and distal pyloric sphincters.  Circular muscle between these + the sphincters themselves form muscular knob Òpyloric torusÓ.

Gastric Blood Supply (282-3)

Mainly coeliac axis; some supply proximally from aorta via left gastric.  Vessels richly anastomotic at mucosal, submucosal and intramuscular levels.

-           fundus & upper left greater curve receive short gastrics from splenic artery in gastrosplenic lig

-           rest of greater curve by gastroepiploic aÕs: right is closer to stomach than left; in partial gastrectomy, supply is divided above left and below right; because left gastro-omental branches are larger, the supply to the omentum is usually preserved.

Venous Drainage (290)

Veins parallel arterial supply, drain to portal or splenic and SMV.  L gastric vein encountered before artery from inferior approach.  Prepyloric vein ¨ right gastric vein.

Lymphatic Drainage and R2 gastrectomy (295)

There is free anastomosis of lymph vessels in the stomach wall, but valves direct flow (see Last p245): watershed parallel to greater curvature; Drains to 4 main groups:

i)                       L gastric: drains all of lesser curve

ii)                     Pancreaticosplenic nodes drain high gr curvature (to splenic hilum and along splenic vein)

iii)                   R gastroepiploic drains greater curve (¨ pyloric nodes near gastroduodenal artery)

iv)                   Pyloric nodes drain pylorus (¨ hepatic nodes)

Gastro-omental nodes may extend several cm into omentum \ omentectomy is included. 

Everything ends up in coeliac nodes eventually; may spread up thoracic duct to TroiserÕs sign in the L supraclavicular fossa.

Nerve supply (see 301)

Sympathetics (vasomotor) and pain together run with arterial branches

Parasympathetic more important; controls motility and secretion (and reflexes Ð 90% of fibres)

Anterior Vagal Trunk (301)

Contains mainly L vagal nerve fibres from oesophageal plexus in posterior mediastinum.  Usually one trunk at the hiatus; may be 2-3 (<10%).  Two divisions:

(1) greater anterior gastric nerve (of Laterget) runs 1cm away from lesser curve in lesser omentum with left gastric artery ¨ 1-12 branches to stomach, usually terminating on antrum.

(2) hepatic branches (1-2) arises just below hiatus ¨ plexuses on hepatic artery / portal vein via lesser omentum; then turns down anterior wall of lesser omentum to reach pylorus.; division of this branch ¨ problems with gastric emptying. 

Anterior trunk lies to L in thorax ¨ comes to lie on R at hiatus.  Palpable as a bowstring in oesophagus when this is placed under tension. 

Posterior Vagal Trunk (301)

Less variable than anterior trunk; formed from mainly R fibres of oesophageal plexus.  Lies further to R than anterior trunk.

Divides below diaphragm ¨ 1 branch runs backwards along L gastric artery to coeliac plexus, and greater posterior gastric nerve which runs in lesser omentum behind anterior trunk to reach antrum. 

Vagotomy Operations

Truncal Vagotomy

= total abdominal vagotomy.  Usually post trunk bigger than anterior one. 

Early experience of TV:  1/3 ¨ impaired gastric emptying requiring drainage procedure.

1/3 ¨ impaired gastric emptying improving with time.

1/3 ¨ normal gastric emptying.

Selective Vagotomy

Denervates stomach, leaves innervation of rest of abdominal cavity intact.

Anterior trunk is divided below hepatic division, post trunk below coeliac division.

Incidence of impaired gastric emptying 10% (? = those people in whom pyloric nerve supply is via anterior nerve of Laterget).

Highly selective vagotomy

Avoids stasis by cutting only branches to fundus and body.  Any individual nerves are found and cut, and ligating vessels also catches nerves.  Possible because arterial branches run in lesser curve transversely while nerve branches approach it obliquely.

Duodenum (261)

10in long (2+3+4+1) tube lying in front of IVC and aorta. C-shaped loop around head of pancreas.

First 2.5cm is between peritoneum of lesser and greater omentum (rest is retroperitoneal)

1st Part (5cm) Superior; L1 level

Runs up, back & to R.  Overlies gastroduodenal artery (284), bile duct, portal vein and beneath = IVC.  

D1 often adherent to neck of GB ( lies over its ÔcapÕ) & is common site of biliary-enteric fistula. 

Next 2.5cm passes back and up on upper part of head of pancreas to medial border of R kidney (261)

-           covered in front by peritoneum, and inferior surface of liver lies over this.

2nd Part (7.5cm) Descending; L2 level (though frequently lower in life)

Curves down over hilum of R kidney with head of pancreas snug inside.  Anterior aspect crossed by transv mesocolon (so half in infracolic, half in supracolic) (261)

Posteromedial wall contains ampulla of Vater at halfway; minor papilla enters 2cm cephalad.  (285)

3rd Part (10cm) Horizontal; L3 level

Curves forwards from R paravertebral gutter over R psoas, gonadal vessels, R ureter, IVC and aorta SMA emerges over it, and mesentery of small intestine cross anteriorly (so that it is in both right and left infracolic compartments; D3 hardest to mobilise due to these anterior relationships. 

4th Part (2.5cm) Ascending; L2 level

Ascends to L of aorta, over L psoas, L lumbar sympathetic trunk (250) to reach lower border of pancreas.  Covered by peritoneum and coils of jejunum.

Breaks free from peritoneum to form duodenojejunal flexure, pulling up a mesentery of small intestine; Ôthe inferior duodenal foldÕ.  Further supported by Ligament of Treitz (a thin band of smooth muscle descending between right crus of diaphragm behind pancreas (in front of aorta and renal vessels) to blend with its outer muscle coat. 

Internally thrown into folds plicae circulares or valvulae conniventes, but first 2.5cm are smooth at duodenal cap (smooth on barium swallow)

Paraduodenal recess lies beneath upper end of IMV; herniation ¨ IMV thrombosis (253)

Inferior and Superior duodenal recesses formed by folds of peritoneum raised between floor of infracolic compartment and lower & upper convexities of D-J flexure.  Retroduodenal recess may form between these. 

Mouths of recesses all face towards each other cf caecal recesses face away; herniae may spread to involve all 4 sites.

 

Blood supply: superior and inferior pancreaticoduodenal arteries (283)

But first 2cm (usual site of ulceration) by hepatic, gastroduodenal, supraduodenal, right gastric, and right gastroepiploic branches.

Veins ¨ superior mesenteric and portal veins (290)

Lymph ¨ with superior and inferior pancreaticoduodenal vessels ¨ coeliac and SMA nodes (295)

Begins L of L1-2 ¨ oblique downward path to R S-I joint, crossing D3/D4, aorta, IVC, R ureter.

Entirely by SMA (branches: inferior p-d, middle colic, , R colic, ileocolic & to 15 intestinal branches).  Intestinal branches form vascular arcades ¨ vasa recta (end arteries; no intramural plexus cf stomach).  If > 2-4cm of small bowel excluded from blood supply ¨ colour change.

MeckelÕs Div & other abnormalities

MeckelÕs: rule of 2Õs: 2% of people, 2 feet from caecum, 2in long (though in practice length variable and site may be more proximal).  Is intestinal end of vitello-intestinal duct, apex may adhere to umb.

-           heterotopic gastric mucosa in 10%, pancreatic mucosa less common.

-           has rudimentary mesentery with blood supply via ileal branch of SMA.

Colon (287)

Features typical of GI Tract:

Serosa, outer longitudinal muscle, inner circular muscle, submucosa, mucosa.

Distinctive features of colon: 

(1) Variability of peritoneal covering:  Asc and desc colon retroperitoneal; transverse and sigmoid colon has mesentery and is invested with peritoneum. 

(2) Appendices epiploicae:  fatty projections from wall.

(3) Thickenings of longitudinal muscle layer form 3 taeniae:  taenia libera (anterior), taenia mesocolica & taenia omentalis (posterior); the circular muscle is in-between; gathers colon into haustra.

Caecum (264)

Invested in peritoneum in majority

-           (note caecal folds on plate 264 which form the retrocaecal recess);  retroperitoneal in a few

Av size = 6cm deep, 8cm wide.  Ileum enters posteromedially 2cm above appendiceal lumen.

Ileocaecal valve has medial and lateral frenula which can be large and mimic a tumour (these transverse lips have only weak sphincter effect).

Blood supply derived from colic branch of ileocolic artery ¨ gives off asc branch, then anterior and larger posterior branches (anterior branch may run in fold in front of ileocaecal junction).

Appendicular artery comes from one of these (more often posterior) or directly from ileocolic artery.

Deep is the peritoneal floor, iliacus and psoas fascia, and femoral and lat fem cutaneous nerves (Pl250).

Lymph ¨nodes along ileocolic artery.

Appendix (264)

From posteromedial wall of caecum (constant), 2cm inferior & posterior to I-C valve. (McBurney's)

Structurally similar to large bowel except many lymphoid masses so irregular lumen.  ~6-10cm long.

Positions: retrocaecal (65%), subcaecal (2.3%), pelvic (31%), paraileal (1%), retro-ileal (0.4%).

-           in the infant the caecum is conical and the appendix hangs downwards, but the lateral wall grows faster than the medial wall, hence its adult position.

Appendix is part of caecum \ has no true mesentery; appendicular artery (from ileal branch of ileocolic artery). Runs in peritoneal fold  (= mesoappendix); is an end-artery.

Bloodless fold of Treves (peritoneal fold) runs anterior to appendix to ileum;

-           the three tinea coli meet to make up its entire longitudinal muscle layer;

Lymph: as for caecum.

Recesses: note on Plate 264 the retrocaecal recess, also caecal folds, which hide the ileocaecal recesses.

Ascending Colon (267)

15cm. Usually retroperitoneal.  Variations from fully mobile on mesentery (embryonic position, 10%) to binding down with JacksonÕs veil. 

Relations: quadratus lumborum, psoas posteriorly.  (Upper part has lower pole R kidney and duodenum posteriorly, liver & GB anteriorly. Lies on iliac fascia and anterior lumbar fascia; medial is the infracolic compartment (see Last 229); lateral is the paracolic gutter.  (292)

Blood: Asc branch of ileocolic below, R colic above.  R colic most variable of colic vessels: 30% from middle colic, 20% from SMA, absent in 50%.  (287)

Relations: R hepatic flexure overlies anterior right kidney, is in contact with inf surface of liver.

Transverse Colon, Greater Omentum (267)

45cm.  Invested in peritoneum, on a mesentery.

Connected to stomach (lies in its curvature) by the gastrocolic omentum (part of greater om.)

-           omentum has no direct developmental relation to colon \ Òbloodless planeÓ exists between it & TC; the rest of the greater omentum appears to Ôhang downÕ off its surface.

-           omentum vessels arise from arcade between R gastric and R gastroepiploic arteries.  Omentectomy for R2 gastrectomy should excise omentum back to colon. 

-           R edge of omentum finishes at D1 ¨ runs up as free edge of lesser omentum.  L aspect of omentum is continuous with gastrosplenic ligament. (see Pl 255)

Middle colic artery arises from SMA just below pancreatic head.  Divides into R & L branches (second L branch present in 40%).  (287)

Relations: hangs anterior to small bowel; mesentery origin runs from inferior pole of right kidney, across descending duodenum and pancreas, to inferior pole of left kidney (Pl 261).

Descending Colon (267)

<30cm. Retroperitoneal in most (mesentery in 20%).

Relations: TC may lie anterior to upper part.  Splenic flexure overlies lower pole L kidney (in contact with spleen and pancreas above 291). Posterior = ureter, QL and iliopsoas (lumbar & iliac fascias) 257.

-           phrenicocolic ligament attaches splenic flexure to diaphragm at 10-11 rib levels (257)

Blood (Pl 287): inferior mesenteric artery (arises from L anterolateral aspect of aorta 3cm above bifurcation, at L3.

-           IMA gives off L colic and sigmoid arteries then continues as superior rectal artery.

-           Note on Pl 287 how whole colon has a marginal artery; this is weakest at the L colic flexure (ie where hindgut and midgut arteries meet)

-           This is supplemented by collateral Arc of Riolan in 10% (from MCA in base of mesentery).

-           L colic branch divides into asc and desc branches (and transverse branch in 25%).

-           L colic absent in 10%; instead accessory L colic from SMA (limits mobilisation of flexure). 

Sigmoid Colon (267)

<45cm (greatly variable).  Invested in peritoneum, hangs on a mesentery coiled anterior to rectum.

Tinea coalesce into a circumferential band; appendices epiploicae are better developed.

Root of sigmoid mesentery = inverted ÒVÓ; apex overlies bifurcation of L common iliac artery and the ureter (on top of the artery (Pl 311)

-           medial limb runs slopes into sacral hollow, stops at S3, lateral limb runs down over pelvic brim along iliac vessels halfway to inguinal ligament (~5cm).

Blood: 2-4 sigmoid branches of inferior mesenteric.

Lymphatics

Epicolic nodes (along colonic wall) ¨ paracolic (along marginal artery) ¨ intermediate (named by branches of SMA & IMA with which they run) ¨ principal (superior & inferior mesenteric nodes).

High ligation for R colonic resection not feasible as some lymphatics run directly back to SMA. 

Nerve Supply

Sympathetic (vasoconstrictor): T10-L2 via coeliac & superior mesenteric ganglia (midgut); lumbar part of sympathetic trunk (hindgut).

Parasympathetic:  Same watershed; vagus above, pelvic splanchnics below.

Pain: runs with sympathetic, except in the rectum some appear to pass with parasympathetics.

Colectomy

Resections are planned according to blood supply and if for cancer are taken close to parent artery for maximal lymph removal.

Right: terminal ileum joined to proximal TC

Transverse: flexures are removed (so is greater omentum)

Left: left end of transverse to part of sigmoid (inferior mesenteric itself may be taken)

Sigmoid: Lower descending to rectum
