Surgical Anatomy : Rectum
 1.  Rectus (Lat) = straight.
- hardly so in human
- 12cm (males) or 13cm (fems) long
- has no mesentery.


2. Continuation of Sigmoid
Defined anatomically by:
i) cessation of mesocolon
ii) cessation of colonic haustra
iii) blending of lateral & antimesenteric taeniae to form a flat anterior muscular band.
- Lasts: gradual distinction from 3rd piece of sacrum (~6cm from sacral promontory).
Therefore rectum has:
i) complete muscle coat (taeniae coalexce)
ii) no sacculations
iii) no mesentery, and no appendices epiploicae.

3. Practical 'surgical' definition of the rectum (Jamieson):
- stretch rectum lightly against promontory of the sacrum.
- upper limit is 15cm from the anal verge using a rigid siggy
- the distal 3cm of this is the anal canal.
- hence anatomical rectum is 12cm long, divided into thirds for planning surgery.

4. Three lateral curves
Correspond to rectal valves (of Houston - ?role to do with flatus discrimination) Pl 365
- upper and lower curves are convex right; middle curve is convex left.
- they are produced by circular muscle layer (c.f. circular folds of small bowel)
- Middle is largest, projects in from right wall at level where peritoneum reflects to form the rectovesical/rectouterine pouch; ie 8cm from anal orifice.
