Left Hemicolectomy
Indications
Splenic flexure tumour?
Argument is L hemi / segmental resection dictated by blood supply vs extended R hemi.
There is no 'right' answer.
I perform an extended R hemi in most situations, as vascularity of anastomosis more assured, better healing of small bowel on large.
- particularly if an obstructed neoplasm
But in a younger or fit patient I would consider a simple segmental resection as a safe option while preserving more colon.

Special Preparation
Bowel prep is not routine but I will use if passage of circular stapler is anticipated to pass through rectum.
Note that don't anastomose onto the sigmoid colon. Often the IMA is compromised and blood supply tenuous.
- anastomose to the upper third of the rectum below the sacral promontory.
Must mobilize the splenic flexure and L half of transverse colon.
Prep
Patient in lithotomy Trendelenburg (Lloyd Davies) position; can alter approach per rectum if required without repositioning
Catheter.
Incision
Standing on the patients right side
- [preferring to hold the tension of the bowel with me rather than allowing the assistant to].
Make a midline incision sufficiently long to access the flexure and pelvis

Procedure
(Described for cancer; obviously would be less radical if benign disease).
Laparotomy inspection by vision and palpation.
- including liver, nodes of retroperitoneum
Handle the tumour as little as possible.
Exteriorize and pack off small bowel to patients right (unrolled pack placed right along the root of mesentery).
Divide sigmoid attachments then descending colon attachments at the mesenteric side of the white line.
Rotate patient to the right, stand between legs, and mobilize splenic flexure
- retrograde dissection along white line to flexure, working in plane
- Assistant then holds up omentum, traction down on a sponge, dissected off transverse mesocolon at midpoint to enter lesser sac, continue to flexure
- dividing the phrenocolic ligament, ligating few vessels inside.
- this is greatly facilitated with a St Mark's retractor, but in experienced hands; avoid damage to the spleen and tail of pancreas.
Elevate the left colon on its mesentery
- dissect free along its embryological plane, identifying and preserving  DJ flexure, left ureter and gonadal vessels
Incise peritoneum over aorta and mobilize IMA to its origin. 
Ligate and divide the artery high at its origin near the aorta using secure double 0-vicryl ties over Robert's
- unless very elderly and clearly unfit, then can preserve the IMA origin but ligate left colonic and sigmoid branches.
Tie the inferior mesenteric vein, which lies slightly laterally, below the inferior border of the pancreas in same manner.
Mobilize sigmoid if anastomosis is to upper third of rectum.
Divide mesentery and marginal vessels to edge of colon at site chosen for resection in transverse colon and rectum or sigmoid.
- i perform a test of the marginal vessel flow.
Place a Furness  clamp across rectum at site of resection.
Irrigate rectum via a catheter through rectum and a providone-iodine solution until cleaned.
Divide distal bowel. Place straight needle prolene sutures through Furness clamp, divide
Place Furness clamp proximally. Divide proximal bowel, remove specimen, and place clamp sutures.
Place 3-0 prolene sutures around Furness sutures to ensure all bowel layers clearly taken up.
Unite bowel ends
- single layered seromuscular inverting suture, big bites simple interrupted 3-0 prolene suture.
- or if able, pass circular stapler (e.g. 28 / 32) through anus and staple proximal and distal ends
--> purse-string around anvil, and distal stapler. Unite ensuring not under tension, well vascularized, no divertics in line, free of mesentery at point of stapling.
Engage, safety off, wind down to closed, fire, unwind 180o, wiggle gently side to side to free, remove.
Wash and close.
No drain.
Post-Op
 Increase diet as tolerated
