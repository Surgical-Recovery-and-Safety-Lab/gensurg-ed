Chapter 81 - Female Hormones
The System
Three hierarchies of hormones:
1. Hypothalamic releasing hormone (GnRH)
2. Anterior pituitary hormones (FSH and LH)
3. Ovarian hormones (oestrogen & progesterone)
Secreted at various amounts through cycle
- FSH and LH and estrogen / progesterone change greatly, GnRH is more constant: pulses every 90 minutes.

The Cycle
Monthly rhythmical change in hormone secretion
- averages 28 days, varies 20-45 (longer = less fertile)

Gonadotropic Hormones
During childhood, no FSH and LH, so ovaries remain inactive.
At age 9-12, pituitary begins to secrete FSH and LH leading to cycling from 11-15 years, with puberty and menarche.
- during each cycle month, cyclical increases / decreases in FSH and LH cause cyclical ovarian changes.
FSH and LH stimulate certain target cells - combine with specific receptors --> change growth, proliferation and secretion.
- almost all result from cAMP activation (2nd messenging, protein kinase, phosphorylations of key enzymes)

Ovarian Follicle Growth
At birth each ovum is surrounded by single layer of granulosa cells = primordial follicle.
- granulsa produces nourshiment for ovum and secretes a maturation inhibiding factor to keep it suspended in prophase of meiotic division.
- when FSH and LH rise in puberty --> growth of follicles.

First stage of follicular growth = moderate enlargement of ovum, with additional layers of granulosa cells being added = primary follicle.
- in first few days of cycle, FSH and LH secreted, though slightly more FSH
- these cause (especially FSH) accelerated growth of 6-12 primary follicles each month
--> rapid proliferation of granulosa, giving many more layers of these; and spindle cells from ovary interstitium collect in layers outside granulosa = theca.
- theca is divided into two layers: theca interna (epithelioid characteristics like granulosa cells; can secrete additional estrogen, progesterone)
- and theca externa: vascular c.t. capsule.
- after this proliferative growth, granulosa cells secrete follicular fluid, containing high concenrations of estrogen --> antrum appears in granulosa mass.

Greatly accelerated growth of primary follicle follows --> vesicular follicles; stimulated by:
1) Estrogen secretion into follicle (positive feedback results as --> granulosa cells to form more FSH receptors --> more sensitive --> more secretion)
2) Pituitary FSH and estrogens combine to promote LH receptors on original granulosa cells --> more rapid follicular secretion.
3) Estrogen and LH together cause more proliferation of thecal cells --> more secretion.
- hence once antral follicles begin to grow, their further growth is explosive
- ovum itself also enlarges 10x (1000x volume)

After a week or more of growth, one follilcle begins to outgrow the others: remaining 5-11 involute = atresia.
- unknown why, but possibly due to estrogen negative feedback on FSH, compensated for in one follicle with positive feedback locally occuring.
- thus only one follicle reaches full potential = mature follicle; 1-1.5cm large.

Ovulation
Day 14 after menstruation onset in a normal 28 day cycle.
Protruding outer wall of follicle swells, central stigma protrudes, fluid begins to ooze out of the follicle through this.
- viscous fluid evaginates, carrying the ovum with it, carried by a mass of several thousand granulosa cells = corona radiata.

LH & Ovulation
LH is needed for final follicular growth & Ovulation.
Otherwise even if large amounts of FSH are available, follicle will not progress to obulation.
- 2 days before ovulation, secretion of LH increases markedly, 6-10x, peaking at 16 hours before ovulation.
- FSH also increases, but only 2-3x at same time
- FSH and LH act synergistically --> rapid swelling of follicle during last few days before ovulation.
LH also specifically converts theca and granulosa cells to progesterone secreting cells
- rate of estrogen secretion falls about 1 day before ovulation
- increasing progesterone is then secreted.
This leads to rapid follicular growth, diminished oestrogen secretion and beginning of progesterone secretion.
- without the preovulatory LH surge, ovulation will not occur.
See Figure 81-3 (G&H p 931)

Initiating Ovulation
LH surge --> steroid secretion (progesterone)
--> theca begin to secrete proteolytic enzymes from lysosomes: dissoves capusualr wall, leading to follicular swelling and degeneration of stigma.
--> rapid growth of new vessels into follicle wall, PGs secreted
- together these factors --> follicular rupture
--> evagination of ovum

Corpus Luteum
During first few hours, remaining granulosa and theca cells --> lutein cells.
Enlarge with lipid inclusions (leteinisation) --> corpus luteum.
- a well developed vascular supply grows into this.
Granulosa cells develop extensive intracellular smooth ER - forms progesterone and estrogen
Theca cells form mostly androstenedione and testosterone (mainly converted by granulosa cells into female hormones anyway)
Luteum grows to 1.5cm peaking at 7-8 days after ovulation.
- then involutes, loses secretory function and becomes a pale corpus albicans, replaced by c.t. over weeks, absorbed entirely over months.

Luteinising function of LH
Change of granulosa / theca cells depends on LH (hence its name)
Local hormone theorised (luteinisation-inhibiting factor) seems to hold luteinisation in check until after ovulation.

Secretion by corpus luteum = additional function of LH
Corpus luteum makes both progesterone and oestrogen in large amounts
Once LH surge acted on granulosa and theca cells to cause luteinisation, lutein cells go through a preordained sequence:
- proliferation
- enlargement
- secretion
- degeneration
Occurs in around 12 days (unless chorionic gonadotropin; similar to LH function) acts to maintain corpus luteum.

Involution of Luteum, Next Cycle
Estrogen (especially) and progesterone have strong feedback effects on anterior pituitary gland: maintains low secretion rates of FSH and LH.
Inhibin from luteal cells also acts on anterior pituitary - reducing FSH secretion especially.
FSH and LH together cause involution of corpus luteum, ending at day 12 of luteum (day 26 of cycle), 2 days before menstruation.
- sudden sessation of estrogen, progesterone and inhibin removes feedback inhibition on FSH and LH
FSH and LH thus initiate new follicles --> new cycle.
Estradiol and Progesterone

Estrogens mainly promote proliferation and growth of specific cells --> secondary sexual characteristics
Progestins devoted to final preparation of uterus for pregancy and breasts for lactation.

Chemistry of Sex Hormones

Estrogens
Three are found in significant quantities: beta-oestradiol, estrone and estriol.
- ovaries secrete beta-oestradiol.
- small amounts of estrone derive from androgens from adrenal cortices or theca cells converted by peripheral tissues.
- estriol is weak; oxidative product of both above hormones, mainly derived in liver.

Progestins
By far most important is progesterone.
Small amounts of 17-alpha-hydroxyprogesterone also secreted; same effect but far less important.

Synthesis of Estrogens and Progestins
All steroids.
Synthesised in ovaries in cholesterol and a little from acetyl coenzyme A
Progesterone and male hormones synthesised first, then still in ovaries, all testosterone and most progesterone --> estrogens by granulosa cells.
- in luteal phase, too much progestrone is produced for conversion, hence its general circulation.

Transport of Estrogens and Progesterone
Both bound with albumin and specific binding globulins.
Loose: rapidly released to tissues over 30m or so.

Fate of Estrogens
Liver conjugates them to form glucuronides and sulfates; 1/5 excreted in bile; remainder in urine.
Also converts estradiol and estrone to estriol (totally impotent)
- hence liver impairment can lead to hyperestrinism.

Fate of Progesterone
Degraded to funcitonless steroids within minutes, again mainly by liver.
Most --> pregnanediol; 10% of original progesterone excreted in urine in this form.

Functions

1. Estrogen on Uterus and Sex Organs
Secretion increases from minute to plenty at puberty --> adult sexual characteristics.
Ovaries, fallopian tubes, vagina and uterus increase in size x several, as do external genitalia wih deposition of fat in mons pubis and labia majoria
- minora also enlarge.
Vaginal epithelium changes from cuboidal to stratified type: resistant to trauma and infection.
Endometrium and its glands enlarge --> aids in nutrition of implanting ovum.

2. Estrogen and Fallopian Tubes
Gland tissue proliferates, ciliated epithelial cells increase in number.
Cilia actiity increases to propel ovum to uterus.

3. Estrogen & Breasts
Primordial female breast = male breast; male under influence of estrogens at early stage can produce milk.
Development of stromal, ductal and fat tissue all influenced by oestrogen.
- lobules and alveoli of breast develop partly under estrogen alone, require prolactin and progesterone for ultimate function.

4. Estrogen & Bones
Increase osteoblastic activity, hence bone growth increases in girls at puberty.
- however also --> uniting of epiphyses with shafts of long bones
- much stronger effect than parallel effect of testosterone in males.
--> hence females end up shorter than males (female eunichs are tall)
After menopause, no oestrogens are secreted.  Deficiency causes:
- diminished osteoblastic activity
- decreased bone matrix
- decreased deposition of bone calcium and phosphate.
--> if severe, leads to osteoporosis.

5. Estrogen and Protein Deposition
Slight total increase in body protein --> slight nitrogen balance +ve.
- due to growth promoting effect of estrogen on bones, sex organs, other tissues.
- testosterone has a more general and powerful effect.

6. Estrogen & body metabolism and Fat Deposition
Slightly increase whole metabolic rate, one third as much as testosterone.
- deposition of increased fat and subcut tissue: thus overall specific gravity of female body is less.
--> Female figure

7. Estrogens & Hair Distribution & Skin
Do not greatly affect it; androgens from female adrenal glands are mainly responsible.
Skin develops a soft smooth texture (but still thicker than the child's / castrated females)
- and becomes more vascular leading to easier bleeding and more warmth.

8. Estrogen & Electrolyte Balance
Sodium and water retention.
- normally slight and rarely of significance; unless in pregnancy: trememendous oestrogens may --> retention.

Functions of Progesterone

1. Progesterone & uterus
Most important: promotes secretory changes in uterine endometrium.
- prepares uterus for implantation of fertilised ovum.

2. Progesterone and Fallopian Tubes
Promotes increased secretion by mucosal lining --> nutrition of fertilised dividing ovum.

3. Progesterone and breasts
Promotes development of lobules and alveoli of breasts --> proliferation, enlargement and secretory nature.
Milk is secreted only after prolactin stimulation.
Progesterone also causes breasts to swell - partly due to secretory development in lobules and alveoli.

Monthly Endometrial Cycle
Associated with ovarian cycle.
First: proliferation
Second: Development of secretory changes
Third: Desquamation of endometrium (menstruation)

Proliferative Phase
Menstruation --> thin layer of endometrial stroma remains
- only epithelial cells remaining in deeper portions.
Estrogens --> stromal and epithelial cells proliferate rapidly
- surface re-epithelialises within 4-7 days.
- thickens progressively over next 10 days.
- at ovulation is 3-5mm thick
Endometrial glands secrete a thin stringy mucus: form longitudinal channels that help guide sperm from vagina to uterus.

Secretory Phase
After ovulation, progesterone and estrogen together secreted by corpus lutem
Estrogens --> slight additional proliferation of endometrium
Progesterone --> marked swelling and secretory development
- glands increase in tortuosity, an excess of secretory substances accumulate in epithelial cells
- cytoplasm of stromal cells increases: lipid and glycogen deposits increase greatly in stromal cells
- blood supply further increases in proportion to increasing secretion --> tortuous vessels.
- after 1 week of ovulation, endometrium is 5-6mm thick.
Highly secretory endometrium is a nutrient store for implantation of fertilised ova during latter half of monthly cycle.
- from time fertilised ovum enters cavity from tube (3-4 days after ovulation) until implantation (7-9 days after) secretes 'uterine milk' to support it.
- thereafter trophoblastic cells invade and begin digesting the endometrium, using its nutrients.

Menstrual Phase
Corpus luteum suddenly involutes 2 days before end of cycle.
Withdrawal of estrogens and progesterone.
--> menstruation; first by decreased stimulation of endometrial cells; followed by rapid involution of endometrium itself.
- tortuous vessels then become vasospastic probably due to effect of involution and release of vasoconstrictors (eg PGs)
- necrosis results from vasospasm, withdrawal of hormones and decrease in nutrients
--> blood seeps into vascular layer, haemorrhagic areas grow, necrotic layers separate, desquamate.
- with contractile stimuus of PGs and other substances, uterine contents are expelled.
40ml of blood and 35ml of serous fluid are thus lost.
- normally the fluid does not clot due to fibrinolysin release along with necrotic endometrial material.
- excessive bleeding --> less fibrinolysin so clotting can occur; a pathological feature.
In 4-7 days, loss of blood ceases; endometrium is re-epithelialised.

Leukorrhea
Lots of WBCs are released with necrotic material, making the uterus highly resistant to infection.
Extreme protective value.
Some liberated substance causes this.
Regulation of the Rhythm

Role of Hypothalamus
GnRH released from hypothalamus
- in pulses lasting 5-25 minutes every 1-2 hours; would not function if released continuously.
- primarily mediobasal hypothalamus involved, especially arcuate nuclei.
- higher neuronal centres transmit signals to arcuate area to modify intennsity of GnRH release and frequency of pulses (psychic factors important)

Negative Feedback
Estrogen inhibits LH and FSH; when progesterone available, inhibitory effect of estrogen multiplied: progesterone has less effect.
- mainly via direct action on anterior pituitary, lesser extent on hypothalamus (altering frequency of pulses)
Inhibin from corpus luteum has same inhibitory effect on FSH and LH to lesser extent as in male
- may be important in decreasing secretion of LH and FSH at end of female cycle.

Positive Feedback
Before ovulation there is a surge in LH (and much smaller surge in FSH)
--> rapid growth of ovarian follicles and accelerated secretion of estrogens.
Abrupt surge is caused by unknown factors:
- probably at this point estrogen has a peculiar positive feedback effect on stimulating FSH and LH
- granulosa cells begin secreting small quantities of progestrone slightly earlier: this may have a role.

Feedback Oscillation
Three successive events

1. Postovulatory secretion of ovarian hormones and depression of pituitary gonadotropins
- corpus luteum secretes hormones, FSH and LH suppressed

2. Follicular growth phase
- before menstruation, corpus luteum regressed --> FSH and LH increase again --> growth occurs
At day 11-12 rates of secretion of ovarian hormones increasing again so that FSH adn LH decrease slightly.
- then strangely comes the surge

3. Surge
Some positive feedback.

Anovulatory Cycles
If the LH surge is insufficient ovulation will not occur (anovulatory cycle)
- variations of cycle continue, but altered: first corpus luteum does not develop
- cycle is shortened by several days; rhythm continues
These occur immediately after puberty and for several months to years before menopause.

Puberty and Menarche
Gradual increase in gonadotropic hormone up to puberty, beginning around age 8 --> menstruation by age 11-16.
- in female, infantile pituitary gland and ovaries capable of full function if stimulated.
- however hypothalamus does not secrete the GnRH in childhood; unclear why; perhaps limbic system maturation.

Menopause
At age 40-50, sexual cycles become irregular and ovulation fails.
- cycles then cease altogether.
Burning out of ovaries is responsible, about 400 primoridal follicles grow into mature follicles and ovulate during reproductive life.
By age 45 few are left: hence FSH and LH has little effect.
When estrogen production falls below a critical level, FSH and LH are produced in unrestrained quantities
Withdrawal of hormone causes marked changes, eg hot flushes, psychic sensations, irritability, fatigue, anxiety, psychosis, decreased stregth and calcification.
- hormone replacement can be effective.
Abnormalities

Hypogonadism
Eg poorly formed ovaries, genetic enzyme defects --> nonfunctional (femal eunuchism) --> infantile organs, no secondary characteristics, grow taller.
- if ovaries removed from a developed woman --> sexual organs regress to infantile size, vagina shrinks and becomes thin, breast atrophy and pubic hair thins.
- same changes occur after menopause.

Irregularity of menses
Quantity of estrogens must rise above a critical value for rhythmical cycling.
Hypogonadism, hypothyroidism are causes.
Prolonged cycles are frequently failed ovulations, perhaps insuffiecient LH at preovulatory surge was missing.

Hypersecretion from Ovaries
Rare because negative feedback.
Usually a feminising tumour eg granulosa cell tumours.
--> irregular bleeding of endometrium = main sign
Female Sexual Act

Stimulation
Depends on psychic and local stimulation
Sexual thoughts --> disire, very much dependent on background training and physiologic drive; though also in proportion to sex hormone levels
- ie peaks around ovulation.
Sexual sensations transmitted through sacral cord via pudendal nerve and sacral plexus --> cerebrum.
- local reflexes integrated at sacral and lumbar levels --> some reactions of the female.

Erection and Lubrication
Erectile tissue like penile tissue is found around introitus and into clitoris.
- controlled by PNS via sacral plexus.
In early phases of stimulation PNS --> dilate arteries of erectile tissue (ACh, NO, VIP at nerve endings)
--> rapid accumulation of blood in erectile tissue --> introitus tightens around the penis --> stimulation of the male.
PNS also --> Bartholin's glands under labia minora --> mucous secretion by these and vaginal epithelium --> lubrication.

Orgasm
At maximal intensity, supported by psychic conditioning --> orgasm reflex.
Analogous to emmision and ejaculation of mail, and probably helps fertilisation:
- perineal muscles contract rhythmically due to cord reflexes --> uterine and fallopian mobility
- and may dilate the cervical canal for up to 30 minutes.
Posterior pituitary --> oxytocin; increases contraction of uterus --> improves sperm transport.
Causes widespread muscle tension, gives way to sense of relaxed peacefulness = resolution.
Fertility

Ovum is viable probably for 24 hours only.
Sperm must be available soon.  Intercourse must occur 4-5 days before ovulation to up to a few hours before ovulation
- thus females only fertile for 4-5 days of their cycle.
Rhythm method of contraception relies on avoiding this time: avoid intercourse 4 days before calculated day of ovulation and 3 days afterward.
- only when periodicity of menstrual cycle is regular.
- in a normal 28 day cycle, ovulation will occur on 13-15th day after onset of cycle.

The pill
Estrogen or progesterone --> inhibits ovulation via preventing LH surge.
See text.

Sterility in females
1:6-8 marriages infertile; 60% female related.
- occasionally no anatomical abnormality; probably physiological
- failure to ovulate is main reason: hyposecretion of gonadotropic hormones  no surge.
In the absence of progesteronic effects can assume cycles are anovulatory.
- can chart body temp: progesterone increases temp in latter half of cycle by 0.5F.
Lack of ovulation by hyposecretion by pituitary can be treated by synthetic administration - chorionic human gonadotropin same as LH
--> ovulation, however can lead to mutiple births.
Endometriosis is another cause, as is salpingitis.
Abnormal mucus secretion by cervix may occur --> hostile environment or plugging
