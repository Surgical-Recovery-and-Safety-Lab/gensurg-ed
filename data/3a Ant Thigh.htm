3.1 : Anterior Thigh

General Plan

Body weight and the thrust of movement is transmitted through the acetabulum of the hip bone.

Sitting weight is transmitted to the ischial tuberosities.

Hip movement is more restricted than shoulder, but good extension / flexion / abduction are possible.

-           knee and ankle are mainly flexion / extension.

Thigh is divided into anterior (extensor), medial (adductor), posterior (flexor)

-           note the lower limb rotates medially during development cf arm groups.

The gluteal region lies higher behind the pelvis and hip.

The leg has three compartments: anterior (extensor), lateral (peroneal), posterior (flexor)

Anterior compartment of thigh

Subcutaneous tissues

Contains cutaneous nerves, lymph, termination of GSV and FA tributaries.

Superficial nerves (463)

Cutaneous supply to thigh from first 3 lumbar nerves via lumbar plexus; all nerves to thigh supply fascia lata as well.   Dermatomes on Pl 507.

(1) Ilioinguinal  L1

= collat. branch of IH.  Emerges through IO to lie on spermatic cord ® pierces external spermatic fascia ® supplies base of penis, anterior 1/3 of scrotum, thigh just below centre of medial inguinal lig.

(2) Genitofemoral nerve L1,2 (femoral L1 only)

Femoral branch entirely cutaneous cf genital branch doesn’t reach skin.

Femoral branch L1 comes off on psoas major ® passes down on EIA & passes beneath inguinal ligament into femoral sheath.  Pierces sheath & fascia lata ® skin over femoral triangle. 

-           genital branch passes through the deep ring with spermatic cord.

(3) Medial femoral cutaneous nerve (L2,3) (Pl502)

Branch of femoral nerve.  Passes medially across femoral vessels, piercing FL ® skin of medial thigh.   Terminal twig reaches patella plexus.

(4) Intermediate femoral cutaneous nerve (L2,3) (Pl502)

Branch of femoral nerve.  Pierces sartorius, FL ® skin of anterior thigh to knee. 

(5) Lateral femoral cutaneous nerve (L2,3) (463, 502)

From lumbar plexus ® passes within thick fascia iliaca near inguinal lig ® passes below or through it 1cm medial to ASIS ® anterior branch (L3; terminal twig ® patella plexus) & posterior branch (L2; supplies from greater trochanter to mid thigh).

Meralgia paraesthetica

Irritation of lat fem cut nerve within tunnel in inguinal ligament (or less commonly in iliac fascia).

-           90% recover spontaneously; neurolysis or division of nerve ® good results in rest.

-           Make 4cm vertical incision from lateral insertion of inguinal ligament distally; need to incise fascia lata to find nerve lying on sartorius.   

(6) Obturator nerve L2-4

(Anterior division of lumbar plexus; emerges medial to psoas major).

Twig from anterior division ® skin & FL over medial condyle of femur.   (503)

Superficial arteries (branches of FA)

These are found in subcut tissues below the inguinal ligament (see Pl 466)

(1) Superficial circumflex iliac

Pierces FA lat to saphenous opening, passes up below inguinal ligament ® ASIS anastomosis. 

(2) Superficial epigastric

Through saphenous opening ® crosses inguinal ligament anteromedially ® superficial abdominal wall tissues.  Venous colleague may form communication between SVC and IVC via thoracoepigastric vein.

(3) Superficial external pudendal

Through saphenous opening.  Passes anterior to spermatic cord (round ligament) after it exits superficial ring ® scrotal (labial) skin. 

(4) Deep external pudendal

Passes between femoral and long saphenous veins, and  runs behind cord ® scrotum (labia).

Superficial veins

= tributaries of long saphenous vein. 

Long (Great) saphenous vein (pl 508)

Longest vein.

Medial marginal vein of foot ® ant to medial malleolus ® passes up one handbreadth behind patella ® spirals medially, passes through cribriform fascia over saphenous opening (3cm below, lat to pubic tubercle) to join femoral vein.

-           contains 20 valves, mostly below knee level.

Tributaries

Usually four veins corresponding to thigh arteries join LSV near SFJ. 

-           deep ext pudendal vein pierces FL over adductor longus (superficial a. between the two veins)

Anterolateral and posteromedial veins of thigh may join LSV just below saphenous opening.

These tributaries help distinguish LSV from FV which has only the LSV itself.

Saphenofemoral junction

Last:  3.5cm below & lateral to pubic tubercle.

Deep external pudendal artery runs medially between LSV and femoral vein. 

Lymphatics (510)

Lymphatic vessels run with LSV ® superficial inguinal nodes. (~10)

-           numerous channels also spiral around the thigh to converge on these nodes

3 groups within it forming T-pattern,  cross-bar along inguinal ligament and stem along LSV:

-           deep fascia of leg & everything superficial ® vertical group;

-           buttock, flank, & back below waist ® lateral group;

-           anterior abdominal wall below umbilicus, perineum (including lower anal canal, external genitalia excluding testes) ® medial group.

Efferent lymphatics pierce cribriform fascia ® external iliac nodes (see 510)

-           lateral and medial also known together as the proximal group.

Deep inguinal nodes (~4) medial to femoral vein, drain deep structures of leg (those that continue with femoral vessels from popliteal fossa).  One or two nodes are in the femoral canal.

-           Cloquet’s node drains glans penis / clitoris

-           Few efferents from superficial inguinal nodes may pass to them

Drain to external iliac nodes through the femoral canal.

Superficial fascia (508)

Scarpa’s fascia extends into thigh just below inguinal ligament ® attaches to FL at flexor crease.  Saphenous opening lies below \ femoral hernia cannot lie beneath Scarpa’s. 

-           these herniae cannot grow large as they emerge into ordinary subcut fat.

Fascia Lata

“Like a stocking who’s top is too large” cut in a slit, overlapped to form saphenous opening (last p111)

Superior attachments

Pubic tubercle ® inguinal ligament ® ASIS ® external lip of iliac crest (splitting to enclose tensor fasciae latae) ® tubercle of iliac crest (splits to enclose gluteus maximus) ® sacrum & sacrotuberous ligament (331) ® ischial tuberosity ®  ischiopubic ramus ® body of pubic bone separating adductors from perinei ® pubic tubercle ® passes laterally deep to itself (as the ‘medial part’) running beneath inguinal ligament along pectineus to insert into pectinate line. 

-           the gap where the two layers overlap = Saphenous opening transmits LSV & is covered in by cribriform fascia (pierced by lymphatics and LSV & tributaries as above)

Inferior attachment

Patella, tibial condyles and head of fibula.  Strengthened by transverse fibres over popliteal fossa ® inserts into deep fascia of calf. 

Tensor fasciae latae (460)

Arises: 5cm external surface of iliac crest between ASIS and tubercle, posterior to sartorius

Inserts: iliotibial tract (thicker here)

Innervation: superior gluteal nerve L4,5 S1.

Action: Pulls tract, assisting glut max to extend the knee joint; also stabilises the pelvis in walking.

Iliotibial tract (460)

Thickening of FL commencing below gr trochanter ® inserts into anterior aspect of lateral condyle of tibia.  Tensor fascia lata and inferior 3/4 of glut max insert into it (these two converge together below the greater trochanter; together = deltoid of hip joint).

Action: maintains KJ in hyperextension with quads relaxed and patella mobile; also constantly used while running when a slightly flexed knee bears the body weight. 

Lateral intermuscular septum

Extends from deep surface of iliotibial tract ® lateral lip of linea aspera.

Forms septum between vastus lateralis and hamstrings ie ant and post compartments.. 

Femoral triangle (458)

Borders: inguinal ligament superiorly (base), medial border of sartorius laterally and medial border of adductor longus medially. 

Floor (gutter): Iliopsoas, pectineus and adductor longus, with femoral neurovascular structures lying in the gutter.  Femoral artery runs from mid-point of base ® apex. 

Clearance of triangle for groin dissection

To medial boundary & apex but lateral to sartorius and well above inguinal ligament. 

Sartorius (458)

Arises: ilium immediately below ASIS ® oblique passage over thigh over roof of adductor canal

Inserts: upper end of subcutaneous border of medial tibia anterior to gracilis & semitendinosus.

Pierced by 2 nerves: intermediate cutaneous nerve of thigh and infrapatellar branch of saphenous nerve.

Action ® “tailor’s position”. (thigh, flexed, laterally rotated and abducted, and knee flexed.

Innervation:  anterior branch femoral nerve (L2,3,4)

Surface marking: line between ASIS and medial condyle of femur. 

Iliacus (462)

Arises: iliac fossa ® runs below inguinal ligament with femoral nerve lying on it. Deep to fem vessels.

Inserts into psoas and femoral shaft just below lesser trochanter.

-           Iliac bursa covers lowest part of iliac fossa, may communicate with hip joint. 

Innervation: femoral nerve L2,3 in iliac fossa.

Action: powerful hip flexor.

Psoas major (462)

Arises: Lumbar spine ® passes below inguinal ligament with femoral artery lying on it

Inserts: rounded tendon inserting into lesser trochanter. 

Innervation: L1,2,3.

Psoas is body wall muscle supplied segmentally cf iliacus is thigh muscle supplied by femoral nerve.

Action: together with iliacus: powerful hip flexor (though unlike iliacus also acts on the spine).

Pectineus (458)

Arises: From pectineal line of pubis ( a quadrilateral muscle)

Inserts: Femur between lesser trochanter & linea aspera. (ie posteroinferior to lesser trochanter)

-           The key to the femoral triangle: covered anteriorly by infolding of FL;  vein and femoral canal lie on the muscle’s surface.

-           FL continues inferiorly here as the medial intermuscular septum which attaches to linea aspera; separates vastus medialis from adductors).

Innervation: anterior division of femoral nerve (L2,3) (branch passes behind femoral sheath)

-           occasionally a twig from obturator)

Action: flexes & adducts thigh.

Femoral sheath (466)

Aorta, IVC and branches lie within endoabdominal fascia and pierce it to enter thigh cf spinal nerves exit intervertebral foramina behind it.

Femoral vessels draw downwards a funnel of transversalis fascia anteriorly and iliac fascia posteriorly to form femoral sheath which fuses with adventitia of artery & vein.

-           these fasciae fuse with inguinal ligament laterally \ femoral nerve runs posterior to sheath.

Femoral canal

A space medial to femoral vein (between it & sheath)

Inlet of canal = femoral ring (inguinal ligament anteriorly, lacunar ligament medially, pectinate line & pectineus posteriorly).

-           the lacunar ligament may need incision to release a strangulated hernia, endangering accessory obturator if present

-           Node of Cloquet lies within canal. 

Femoral artery (466)

Enters thigh at mid-inguinal point (b/n ASIS and pubic symphysis) on psoas major tendon (separates it from this hip joint capsule; this is where it is palpated / cannulated)

® emerges from femoral sheath distally, then disappears below sartorius to enter adductor hiatus. 

Mean length of artery before bifurcation 5cm, range 1-8cm.

Surface marking: line between midinguinal point and medial condyle of femur.

Cannulation: Easily accessible, but risk of retroperitoneal haemorrhage, AV fistula or even gut perf.

Superficial femoral artery (continuation of ‘common’ femoral, can all be known as Femoral)

Covered by roof of subsartorial canal (fascia running from adductors ® vastus medialis) and sartorius. 

Exposure:  upper SFA by retracting sartorius laterally; mid-portion by splitting sartorius, inferior by retracting sartorius posteromedially.    

Profunda femoris artery (P466)

Given off just below femoral sheath from lateral side of femoral.  Supplies all thigh muscles.

-           runs down deep to adductor longus, lying on pectineus then adductor brevis

-           ® eventually embeds in adductor magnus as terminal 4th perforator, posterior to linea aspera.

Average length of PFA 30cm. 

 
Branches (467)

Medial circumflex femoral

Usually from medial PFA (else CFA); passes back b/n pectineus & psoas tendon.

® runs above adductor brevis, below quadratus femoris ® gluteal region® 2 branches:

(1)     Ascending ® trochanteric anastomosis

(2)     Horizontal ® cruciate anastomosis

Lateral circumflex femoral usually from lateral PFA (else CFA) ® passes laterally between branches of femoral nerve dividing them into superficial and deep groups ® passes beyond femoral triangle beneath sartorius to lie deep to rectus femoris.  ® 3 branches:

(1) ascending ® runs up vastus lateralis to meet ASIS to join the anastomosis there (with superficial and deep circumflex iliac, iliac branch of iliolumbar, superior branch of superior gluteal)

-           must be secured b/n sartorius and tensor in the anterior approach to the hip

(2) transverse ® passes across vastus lateralis ® winds around femur ® cruciate anastomosis.

(3) Descending ® steeply down with n. to vastus lateralis in groove b/n lateralis and intermedius.

-           supplies these then sends twigs around knee.

4 muscular perforating branches passing back through adductor magnus;

-           1st & 4th pass above & below adductor brevis, 2nd & 3rd through it. 

-           Supply adductors & hamstrings, anastomose with each other, cruciate above & popliteal below.

Femoral vein

Enters femoral triangle posterior to artery ® comes to lie medially & receives a tributary corresponding to a PFV, then LSV just below the femoral sheath.

-           then runs below the inguinal ligament, to run along pelvic brim as external iliac vein.

-           has 4-5 valves, usually above junctions with profunda and great saphenous.

Femoral nerve (464-6, 502)

Largest branch of lumbar plexus.

Nerve of anterior compartment of thigh formed from posterior divisions of ventral rami of L2-4 (same segments of obturator nerve, which is derived from anterior divisions).

-           (although quads are flexors, they are derived from muscle ‘borrowed’ from the trunk).

-           supplies iliacus in the abdomen, then passes between psoas and iliacus in iliac fossa; passes deep to inguinal ligament at lateral edge of femoral sheath (separates it from femoral artery)

Superficial branches

2 cutaneous (intermed. and med fem cut nerves) and 2 muscular (® sartorius & pectineus) branches.

-           the intermediate cutaneous nerve also pierces sartorius and may supply it

-           nerve to pectineus passes behind the sheath to reach the muscle.

Medial femoral cutaneous branch often injured in redo operations on SFA. 

Deep branches

4 muscular

® rectus femoris (may be double; also passes a slip to the hip)

® vastus medialis (passes lateral to femoral artery into the adductor canal to supply the muscle)

® vastus lateralis (runs down with descending lateral femoral circumflex artery, reaches knee)

® vastus intermedius (enters anterior surface of the muscle, but also runs to knee)

1 cutaneous (saphenous) branch: runs over front of femoral artery in the adductor canal to reach its medial side ® exits distally by passing between sartorius and gracilis. 

Quadriceps femoris (456-8)

Extensor of KJ; largest muscle in body.  In 4 parts converging together to form quadriceps tendon (contains patella) then patella tendon to insert into tuberosity of tibia.

(1) Rectus femoris

Arises: ilium by 2 heads: Reflected head from area above acetabulum, straight head from ASIS

-           these heads unite & rectus forms the ‘anterior lamina’ of quadriceps

-           posterior surface is a glistening aponeurosis; slides on vastus intermedius.

(2) Vastus lateralis

Largest of vastii.  Extensive linear origin:

Arises: Lateral lip of linea aspera & lateral supracondylar line + lateral intermuscular septum

-           lateral edge lies free on vastus intermedius, gutter between contains descending lat circumflex artery together with nerve to vastus lateralis.

(3) Vastus intermedius

Arises: anterior & lateral upper 2/3 of shaft of femur

(4) Vastus medialis

Arises: from medial lip of linea aspera, lower part of intertrochanteric line, medial supracondylar line and partly from the tendon of adductor magnus.   Slopes medially around the femur.

Inserts: lowest fibres are nearly horizontal as they insert into the patella.

Quadriceps Tendon

Attaches to base and sides of patella; & thin sheet runs over top into patella ligament & retinaculum.

Patella Ligament

Patella retinacula are fibrous expansions from the quadriceps tendon, connect sides of patella with lower margins of tibial condyles and sides of patella ligament.  Else inserts into tibial tuberosity.

Articularis Genu

Small, Arises anterior lower femoral shaft, deep to intermedius, inserts into upper suprapatellar bursa.

Innervation all by femoral nerve L3-4

Actions: main extensor of knee; rectus may also help flex hip.

Test: extend knee against resistance.

Stability of Patella

Is a sesamoid.  Patella ligament is vertical but quadriceps is oblique (in line with femur)

-           thus when muscle contracts it tends to pull the patella laterally.

3 factors thus discourage lateral dislocation:

i)                       bone: forward prominence of lateral femoral condyle

ii)                     ligament: tension of medial patellar retinaculum.

iii)                   Muscle: lowest vastus medialis fibres insert horizontally, holding patella medially.

Adductor canal (466) (aka Hunter’s / subsartorial canal)

Gutter-shaped groove between vastus medialis and adductors, roofed in by fascia and then sartorius.

-           floored by adductor longus above, and magnus below.

-           the roofing fascia contains the subsartorial plexus enmeshed in it; receives branches from med cut nerve of thigh, saphenous nerve, and ant division of obturator.

-           The plexus supplies: overlying fascia lata, skin above medial knee

Contents: femoral artery & vein, saphenous nerve, nerve to vastus medialis (upper part only).

-           femoral vessels leave canal by passing b/n hamstring and adductor parts of adductor magnus to enter popliteal fossa.

Distally the femoral vein is posterolateral to the artery, ascends posteriorly then lies medially

-           this is in keeping with the embryological rotation; saphenous nerve also spirals around such that at all stages femoral artery lies b/n saphenous nerve and femoral vein.

Descending genicular artery (Pl 467) arises from femoral just above adductor hiatus

-           ® superficial saphenous (accompanies saphenous nerve) and deep muscular (enters vastus medialis and joins knee anastomosis).

Saphenous nerve exits between posterior border of sartorius and gracilis; pierces FL here, then passes down behind the GSV

-           gives infrapatellar branch just before leaving canal, pierces sartorius, supplies patellar plexus and skin over patella.
