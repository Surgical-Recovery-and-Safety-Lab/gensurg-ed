Principles of abdominal incisions
1. Anatomical access
2. Strength of healing
3. Speed
4. Body habitus
5. Prevision incisions / need for drains / colostomy

Midline incision: principles
Keep scalpel straight
Lateral traction applied
Go around umbilicus to left
- avoids falciform and round ligament of liver.
No need to change blades after skin: no evidence for increased infection rates.
Identify down to linea alba
Sharp incision
- leave 5-8mm of linea alba attached to umbilicus for a good bite when closing.
Preperitoneal fat is more abundant to R of midline (falciform & round lig)
- may mask the superior bladder in the lower abdomen.
- retract this layer to the Right and inferiorly to expose the peritoneum
Lift between clips, avoid bowel, nick, allow bowel to fall away with entry of air.
Extend caudad and cephalad by inserting two fingers and lifting, palpating for adherent bowel.

Breast
Follow Langer's lines, circumareolar is optomal centrally, arcuate peripherally.
Conservative treatments favoured - but need to include old excision should mastectomy become important.
May need radial incisions at inner or outer aspects.
