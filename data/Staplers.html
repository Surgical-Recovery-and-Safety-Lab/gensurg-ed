Stapling and Staplers : Principles
Staple or Sew?
Staplers may be preferred for integrity of join and speed
Speed advantageous in critically-ill patients undergoing emergency operation.
Sutures may be preferred as can tie to appropriate tension
- approximate, but not strangulate thickened bowel.
- e.g. may be difficult to staple safely in Crohn's
But there is no evidence that staples are safer than sutures or vice versa.
Occasional rare instances where cannot get stapler into a deep cavity, then forced to sew.

Staple Characteristics
Enters tissue straight and bends into a B when fired.
- allows blood flow through staple line
Occasionally need a haemostatic figure-8 at bleed point on line.
Two standard sizes available:
- blue (intestine): 3.5 mm long (1.5 mm closed) x 4mm wide
- green (stomach): 4.8 mm long (2mm closed) x 4mm wide
Endoscopic linear cutter compresses tissues to ~1.75 mm
Generally achieve inversion.

Common Staplers
Linear devices
TA
Generally 55mm, 75mm, 90mm options
Good for end of colon stump, duodenal stump
Bowel end, eg dividing off enterotomies.

Linear cutting devices
Inverts
Side-side anastomoses generally.

Circular devices
Lumen ~2-2.5mm depending on device.
Compresses tissues to ~2mm
Ideal for low rectal anastomoses
Causes of Technical Stapling Failure

1. Unfit bowel
- must have a good blood supply.
- avoid narrow ischaemic strips from adjacent staple lines
- do not excessively compress thick tissues (uncommon; e.g chronically obstructed gastric wall to 6-8mm)
--> can get a linear tear next to staples; invert or convert to hand-sewn.

2. Linear tension
- staplers more prone to cut through than sutures under tension.

3. Instrument failure
- make sure that forks are cleanly parallel, else gap will place a distracted weak staple line.
- check the staple line formation after each fire.
- failure of the cutting aspect is rare
- always wipe excess staples away before inserting a new cartridge

4. Operator error
- do not staple over the mesentery of the bowel; intramural haematoma
- do not include mesenteric fat between seromuscular layers
- inspect staple line for gastric bleeding, transfix.
- oversew an excessively bleeding staple line (us. only stomach).
- avoid too much tissue crimped in the stapler.
- avoid false passages
- relax the bowel when stapling (tension), and point forks straight down bowel.

Inspect staple lines carefully after their construction.
